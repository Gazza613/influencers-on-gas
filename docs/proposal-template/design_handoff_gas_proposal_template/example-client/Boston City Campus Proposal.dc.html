01 · Executive Summary
The case in one page

Boston City Campus competes in the strongest structural tailwind private higher education has seen in a generation, and against the best-funded competitors it has ever faced. For 2026 the public system can absorb roughly half of the qualifying matriculants, leaving hundreds of thousands of academically eligible, largely self-funding students looking for an accredited home. The institution that reaches those students first, answers them fastest and qualifies them best wins the enrolment. Boston has the credential, the footprint, the trust and the funding answer: 35 years, roughly 50 support centres in all nine provinces, more than 100 accredited programmes, a rare accreditation stack including ACBSP in the United States and BAC in the United Kingdom, and payment plans plus Capitec education credit that dissolve the self-funding market’s biggest objection. What limits growth is not the offer; it is the operating model that converts interest into enrolment. Enquiries spike twice a year at intake while human follow-up capacity does not, and the institution that answers first usually wins the enrolment. 02 · The Opportunity
A structural shift, not a passing cycle

Every January, a market of hundreds of thousands of qualified, motivated students is turned away from public universities. They do not disappear. They look for an accredited private home, and they look fast. Crucially for Boston’s model, there is little or no state funding for study at private institutions, so this pool is largely self-funding and family-funding: prospects who research carefully, weigh affordability and accreditation, and reward the institution that answers their questions quickly and credibly. Boston already holds the strongest answer in the category: fees spread over 4, 8, 10 or 12 months, or Capitec Credit for Education up to R500,000 paid directly to Boston. The engine’s job is to make sure every qualified prospect hears it before a competitor replies. That is a marketing and qualification contest, and it is precisely the contest this ecosystem is built to win. Boston does not need to manufacture demand; it needs to capture demand the market is generating in unprecedented volume, faster and more intelligently than better-funded competitors.
